Research

Kinetic Simulations

Particle Acceleration

Maximum Energy Cosmic Rays at Supernova Remnant Shocks

I use hybrid kinetic simulations (dHybridR) to study how plasma instabilities at collisionless shocks accelerate particles, with the goal of understanding whether SNRs can reach the "knee" of the cosmic ray energy spectrum. Active research threads include:

  • Developed and validated a "faux-shock" boundary condition that substantially speeds up shock simulations — ApJ
  • Studying particle acceleration driven by cosmic ray streaming instabilities; presented at ICRC 2024 — proceedings, manuscript in preparation
  • Extending this to the high-Mach-number regime, where strong magnetic turbulence becomes highly non-linear.
Particle Tracking & Test-Particle Simulations

Testing the Limits of Diffusive Shock Acceleration Theory

Analytical models of diffusive shock acceleration (DSA) make idealizing assumptions that break down in realistic environments. I use particle tracking in hybrid simulation outputs to directly test where these models succeed and where they fail, comparing numerical particle spectra to DSA predictions across a range of shock conditions.

Plasma Micro-Instabilities

Mirror & Firehose Instabilities and Jitter Radiation in SNRs

In high-β plasmas, pressure anisotropy drives micro-instabilities — mirror and firehose modes — that generate small-scale magnetic fluctuations. I am investigating whether these instabilities can produce the jitter radiation observed in supernova remnants, and what they imply for electron heating and emission in these environments.

Phenomenology

SNR Neutrinos

A Catalog of Hadronic Supernova Remnants for Neutrino Followup

Neutrinos from individual Galactic SNRs are almost always too faint for current instruments, but a stacked analysis may offer a viable detection route. I constructed a catalog of SNRs with hadronic emission signatures, ranked by predicted neutrino flux, to serve as a target list for IceCube and future detectors

BSM Physics

Dark Matter & Beyond-Standard-Model Signatures

Searches for physics beyond the Standard Model using indirect and collider signatures. This includes work on the neutrino flux produced by decays of ultra-heavy dark matter, and an earlier search for delayed and non-pointing photon signatures at the ATLAS detector.
