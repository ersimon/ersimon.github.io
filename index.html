Research

Kinetic Simulations

Maximum Energy Cosmic Rays at Supernova Remnant Shocks

I use hybrid kinetic simulations to study how plasma instabilities at collisionless shocks accelerate particles, with the goal of understanding whether SNRs can reach the "knee" of the cosmic ray energy spectrum.

  • A “Faux-Shock” Method for Hybrid Simulations of Astrophysical Shocks
  • Maximum Energy Achievable in Supernova Remnants: Self-Consistent Simulations
  • The high-Mach-number, strongly non-linear regime (in progress).
Testing the Limits of Diffusive Shock Acceleration Theory

Analytical models of diffusive shock acceleration (DSA) make idealizing assumptions that break down in realistic environments. I use particle tracking and test particle simulations to directly test where these models succeed and where they fail.

Mirror & Firehose Instabilities

In high-β plasmas, pressure anisotropy drives micro-instabilities — mirror and firehose modes — that generate small-scale magnetic fluctuations. I am investigating whether these instabilities can produce the jitter radiation observed in supernova remnants, and what they imply for electron heating and emission in these environments.

Phenomenology

Neutrinos

Neutrinos from individual Galactic SNRs are almost always too faint for current instruments, but a stacked analysis may offer a viable detection route. I constructed a catalog of SNRs with hadronic emission signatures, ranked by predicted neutrino flux, to serve as a target list for IceCube and future detectors

  • Detecting Neutrino Emission from Supernova Remnants: A Theoretically Motivated Target Catalog
BSM Physics

Dark Matter & Beyond-Standard-Model Signatures

Searches for physics beyond the Standard Model using indirect and collider signatures. This includes work on the neutrino flux produced by decays of ultra-heavy dark matter, and an earlier search for delayed and non-pointing photon signatures at the ATLAS detector.
